I've been on the engineering team at Business Insider for over 10 years.

This week, my photos ran on the site.

[![Business Insider's photo story on the 2026 NYC Marathon, with my photo of the elite men's field as the lead image](https://res.cloudinary.com/dt3vcpkj6/image/upload/f_auto,q_auto,w_1400/v1793753317/web_assets/bi-marathon-browser-window-no-ad.png)](https://www.businessinsider.com/see-best-photos-2026-nyc-marathon-celebrities-2026-11)

Business Insider used my shot of the men's pro field as the lead photo in [Tristin Hoffman's story](https://www.businessinsider.com/see-best-photos-2026-nyc-marathon-celebrities-2026-11), and several of my other shots are in the story as well.

Marathon Sunday started for me capturing runners getting off the subway and heading to the Staten Island Ferry. The platform and the plaza were filled with nervous energy and questionable fashion choices.

I spent the rest of the day on the Upper East Side.

First, just off the Queensboro Bridge, I caught the pro fields and a few speedy *celebrities* - [Casey Neistat](https://photos.reubeningber.com/running/tcs-nyc-marathon-2026?photo=20261101-RAI03109) and [Oz Pearlman](https://photos.reubeningber.com/running/tcs-nyc-marathon-2026?photo=20261101-RAI02823). I even captured the race's Grand Marshal [Tracy Morgan](https://photos.reubeningber.com/running/tcs-nyc-marathon-2026?photo=20261101-RAI02671).

Then I went over to 5th Avenue and shot runners making their way up into Central Park around mile 24. There I was able to capture some of my friends and others like the Statue of Liberty.
